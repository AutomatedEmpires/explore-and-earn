## 2. What we collect (§11.2 item 2)

From you: account details, profile, work authorization, availability, applications, messages, photos, and a resume file if you upload one. Automatically: device, log, and coarse location data, and cookies. From others: the sign-in provider, the payment processor, and verification services.

Work authorization is collected on its own step. Hosts see only "authorized" or "needs sponsorship". They do not see a visa type or an end date. We do not use work authorization for ranking beyond the fit score's documented input, for analytics, or for marketing (legal review §2.2 item 4 and §4.2).

## 3. Sensitive information (§11.2 item 3)

Work authorization and precise location are sensitive. We ask before we use either. Work authorization is used to show a host the two answers above when you apply or when you choose to be found. Precise location, for "Near me", is used in the page only. The server receives a map viewport or a position rounded to about a kilometer, and we store nothing (legal review §4.2). We use neither for marketing. If we ever want to store a precise position, we will ask first.

## 4. Why we use it (§11.2 item 4)

We use personal information to run accounts, show listings, deliver applications and messages, score fit as a ranking aid, scan for scams and child-safety hashes, bill hosts, secure the service, and meet legal duties. For EU and UK users, the legal basis for each purpose is the one counsel writes in answer to legal review §13 question 11. Until that answer, this draft does not name a basis.

## 5. Who sees what (§11.2 item 5)

A host sees a pool card only if you opt in: first name, last initial, headline, skills, and availability (legal review §4.3). Pool visibility is off until you turn it on. After you apply, the host sees the frozen application. Contact details come later, when the product's staged reveal says they do. An uploaded resume file is never sent to hosts (FD-2; legal review §4.2). Your minimum pay and the states you prefer stay off the fit explanation, except a matching state. Other seekers do not see your application.

## 6. Automated tools (§11.2 item 6)

We use a fit score to rank pools and sort applicants. It does not decide. We scan messages for scams. We hash-match images for child-safety. AI may propose resume-import fields and writing help; you review every field before it is saved (product design section 18). Our AI provider does not train on your data. You may ask for a human review of a fit score or of an automated support answer.

## 7. Who we share with (§11.2 item 7)

We share information with hosts you apply to or choose to be found by; with service providers on the subprocessor list (hosting, sign-in, payments, email, analytics, error monitoring, maps, AI, malware scanning, and photo hashing); with NCMEC and law enforcement when the law requires it; and with a successor business if one takes over the service. We do not sell personal information or share it for cross-context advertising (legal review §4.3 and §11.2 item 7).

## 8. Your choices (§11.2 item 8)

You can turn candidate-pool visibility and photo visibility off. You can set notification categories. Cookie choices are in the Cookie Policy. A Global Privacy Control signal is treated as a rejection of analytics everywhere, not only in the EU (legal review §4.5; SP8 spec 8.4).

## 9. Your rights (§11.2 item 9)

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, and export your information, to limit the use of sensitive information, and to appeal a refusal. You exercise them in the app. We respond in the time the applicable law sets (one month for EU and UK requests, until counsel says otherwise). There is no penalty for using a privacy right (legal review §4.6).

Some records survive deletion of an account because they are shared or the law requires them: shared application records, safety records, billing records, legal holds, and child-safety evidence for one year after a report (legal review §3.1 and §11.2 item 10). Whether billing records are kept for seven years, and whether a deleted seeker's shared applications are redacted, is still with counsel (SP8 spec section 13).

## 11. Security and breach (§11.2 item 11)

We use access controls, encryption in transit, and a private bucket for files. What we do if there is a breach is the one-page runbook at `docs/runbooks/breach-response.md` (legal review §4.7). A host who takes applications under false pretenses may count as unauthorized acquisition; counsel decides case by case.

## 12. International transfers (§11.2 item 12)

International transfers: the service is operated in the United States. If we accept EU or UK seekers, each US vendor needs a transfer mechanism counsel approves (legal review §4.6 and §13 question 11). Until that decision, this draft does not name a mechanism.

## 13. Children (§11.2 item 13)

The service is for adults. If we learn a user is under 18, we close the account and delete the personal information we are allowed to delete, and we follow the child-safety rules if the report is about a minor at risk (legal review §2.6 and §3).

## 14. Changes (§11.2 item 14)

Changes to this policy follow the same notice as the Terms. A material change takes effect no sooner than 30 days after we schedule it (SP8 spec 8.5). The date and version on the published page change with it.
