# Breach response

Draft for counsel review, not legal advice. One page, from legal review §4.7. A fake host who obtains applications under false pretenses may be unauthorized acquisition under some state laws (§7). Counsel decides that case by case (§13 question 8). Do not put message text, resumes, or documents in the incident notes.

## Who decides

The founder decides whether the incident is a breach that requires notice, with counsel on the call before any notice is sent. An operator may disable the account, revoke sessions, and preserve logs. An operator does not send notice alone.

## How affected users are identified

From application, message, and audit rows: every seeker who applied to or messaged the organization, and every host whose listing or applicant data was in the same access. Record counts and account ids. Record categories of data, not the data itself.

## Notice template

Subject: A notice about your Explore&Earn account

We are writing because personal information connected to your account may have been acquired without authorization. What happened: [one sentence]. What was involved: [categories only]. What we are doing: [containment]. What you can do: change your password, watch for fraud, and use https://reportfraud.ftc.gov and https://www.identitytheft.gov. We will write again if we learn more. Contact: [founder supplies the postal address and email].

## Regulator list

Counsel names the states before any filing. Start with the states where affected people live and the state where the business is. Check each named state's breach-notification statute, including Colorado and California when they are on that list. Add a federal regulator only if counsel names one. Record the time of discovery, the founder's decision, and each notice sent.

## Confirmed fake host

Ban the organization and its payment and device fingerprints, hide its listings, and send the notice above to every seeker who applied or messaged (§7 item 6). Then ask counsel whether the state laws on this page also apply.
